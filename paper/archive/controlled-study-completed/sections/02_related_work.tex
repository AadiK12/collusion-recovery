\section{Related Work}
\citet{shi2026emergent} study repeated peer verification with limited
communication. Their controlled peer interventions test whether agents influence
each other, rather than merely breaking the same rule independently. Their agents
use tools, five message rounds, and reflection after feedback
\citep{saltnlp2026repo}.

We use fixed claims, two message rounds, and saved transcripts. Our scripted
peers send identical messages but make different approval decisions. Feedback
accurately reports those decisions and their scores. This simpler setup lets us
compare recovery across matched histories, but differs from the original
study's task and agent infrastructure.

\citet{mazaheri2026audit} show that earlier audit and repair exchanges can make
verifiers more lenient. \citet{shnaidman2026reset} distinguish correcting an agent
within a conversation from removing the context that influenced it. We test
evidence restoration, correction, and history removal after controlled peer
interaction. Tracking both faulty and correct approvals lets us distinguish
better review from a general tendency to reject.
