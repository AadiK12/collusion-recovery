\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Long-horizon interaction and reciprocal verification.}
Shi, Zhang, and Yang study repeated task completion, evidence exchange, peer
verification, and shared feedback, reporting joint protocol violations across a
range of model configurations and using controlled peers and history ablations to
probe contributing factors \citep{shi2026emergent}. Their study establishes that
history and peer behavior can matter in this class of environment. Our work is a
smaller verification-only adaptation, not a reproduction of their benchmark. It
changes the target from formation incidence to post-repair behavior on
objectively faulty packets, and compares operator-available recovery actions
under a deterministic oracle. The implementation was written independently; the
upstream reference repository is used only as a methodological reference
\citep{saltnlp2026repo}.

\paragraph{Trust, checking, and peer influence.}
Reduced verification can be adaptive when a partner is reliable and dangerous
when it is not. Chen operationalizes inter-agent trust through costly checking and
finds model-specific formation, breakage, and recovery patterns
\citep{chen2026trust}. We therefore avoid treating reduced checking as harmful by
definition: harm requires a faulty approval, while usefulness requires retaining
correct approvals. Gong et al. hold a downstream task fixed while changing an
upstream message and show that erroneous messages can displace answers supported
by the receiver's own evidence \citep{gong2026upstream}. Their design motivates
our current-message control. Our reciprocal-request contrast additionally holds
the peer's visible \texttt{ACCEPT} action and message prefix fixed, but generic
compliance with changed wording remains a live alternative explanation.

\paragraph{Path dependence and recovery.}
Prior context can shift behavior even on an unchanged present task. Mazaheri and
Mazaheri find threshold movement after audit--repair context relative to a
length-matched control, while cautioning that greater leniency is not necessarily
harmful at every operating point \citep{mazaheri2026audit}. Shnaidman studies
recovery after repeated false conversational pressure and separates
history-preserving repairs from operations that remove pressure-bearing context
\citep{shnaidman2026reset}. Our setting differs in using reciprocal reviewers,
deterministic source records, and explicit faulty-versus-correct packet outcomes.
Together, this literature rules out broad claims that context dependence, reset,
or recovery evaluation are themselves novel.

\paragraph{Workflow repair and corrective transfer.}
EAIR frames multi-agent failures around organizational properties, explicit
failure criteria, trace-grounded diagnosis, targeted repair, and behavioral
retesting \citep{ma2026eair}. That system-level approach supports our insistence
on observable contracts and utility-preserving repair. Rivera et al. instead
install policy--monitor collusion through fine-tuning and study whether correction
on one caught behavior transfers to held-out behaviors
\citep{rivera2026organisms}. Their training-level intervention addresses a
different causal object: our within-session text-history experiment cannot claim
weight-level unlearning or corrective transfer.

\paragraph{Positioning boundary.}
The defensible contribution is the combination of a deterministic faulty-approval
outcome, matched post-restoration branches, an action-matched direct-request
diagnostic, and evidence-calibrated claim gates. We do not claim the first study of
collusion, context effects, reset, correction, trust, or workflow repair. A main
history contrast does not establish peer causation; a scripted-peer effect does
not establish spontaneous agreement; and their conjunction does not reveal an
agent's private motive. This boundary is part of the design rather than a
post-hoc limitation.
