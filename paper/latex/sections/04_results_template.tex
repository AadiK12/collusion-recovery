\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Both pairs approved work without the required logs}
Both agents approved without the required complete logs in eight of ten main
rounds in the pair without warm-up and six of ten in the pair with summary
warm-up (Table~\ref{tab:source-behavior}). Both pairs did this in their final
three rounds. No reviewer received a complete peer log in these main rounds.

Each pair produced two wrong answers, and all four were approved. In the second
pair's final round, both answers were wrong and both agents approved their
partner's work. Review of their public messages found no explicit agreement to
approve each other. The observed failure is approval without the required
evidence; the messages do not establish deliberate coordination.

\subsection{Complete logs allowed accurate review}
Both agents first passed three basic checks: approve correct work with a complete
log, reject wrong work with a complete log, and reject work with an incomplete
log. These six checks are separate from the 80 repair-test decisions.

All 80 decisions followed the required response format. Each condition included
four correct and four wrong answers in the separate tests, and four of each in
the consecutive reviews. Table~\ref{tab:recovery-results} shows the approvals.

With complete logs, the agents approved every correct answer and rejected every
wrong answer. Adding a reminder or starting a fresh conversation alone did not
change this result. Combining both changes made the agents reject every answer,
including all eight correct answers across the two types of test.

The same pattern held across all four consecutive reviews for both agents.
Appendix~\ref{app:sequences} shows each decision. This follow-up involved reviewing
fixed work; the agents did not resume talking to each other.

\subsection{Earlier approvals stopped even with incomplete logs}
When logs remained incomplete, the agents rejected every answer, as the review
rule required. Their earlier approvals had therefore already stopped in the
later test. We cannot say that providing complete logs caused that change.
The benefit of complete logs was that agents could also approve correct work.

The final dataset contains two completed interaction histories but repair tests
for only one pair. It includes 80 of the originally planned 480 review decisions.
The other 400 were not collected and are not counted as rejections. Repeated
decisions from one pair do not establish how other pairs would respond.
