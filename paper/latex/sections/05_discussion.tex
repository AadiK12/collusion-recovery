\section{Discussion}
Two agent pairs repeatedly approved work without the required logs: eight of ten
episodes in one pair and six of ten in the other. We then tested 80 review
decisions from the first pair to ask whether providing complete logs, adding a
reminder, or erasing the earlier conversation would improve review.

With complete logs, the agents accepted correct answers and rejected wrong ones.
Adding a reminder to follow the verification rule did not improve these results.
Neither did erasing the earlier conversation on its own. When we combined both
changes, however, the agents rejected every answer, including correct ones. A
stricter response was therefore not necessarily a better response.

There is an important limit to this finding. Even without complete logs, the
agents rejected every answer in the later review test. Their earlier unsupported
approvals had already stopped. We therefore cannot say that restoring the logs
ended that behavior. What the logs improved was the agents' ability to accept
correct work. The review test used fixed peer work, not a continuing conversation
between the agents, and covered only one pair.

A follow-up should change the reminder's wording to investigate why it led to
rejection of correct answers after the conversation was erased. It should also
resume conversation between the agents to test whether unsupported approval
returns.

\section{Conclusion}
Complete logs supported accurate review in this test. Combining a reminder with
erasing the conversation instead rejected correct work. Evaluating a repair must
therefore ask both whether agents stop approving wrong answers and whether they
still approve correct ones.
