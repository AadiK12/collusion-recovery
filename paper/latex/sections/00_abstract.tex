Does agent collusion persist after the conditions that enabled it are changed? We studied two pairs of agents that solved tasks, exchanged short messages, and reviewed each other's work. Both agents approved without the required complete logs in eight of ten rounds in one pair and six of ten in the other. Four wrong answers were approved across the two pairs. We then tested 80 review decisions from the first pair. We compared incomplete logs with complete logs, a reminder to follow the review rule, a fresh conversation, and their combinations. With complete logs, the agents approved every correct answer and rejected every wrong answer. Adding a reminder or starting a fresh conversation alone gave the same results. Combining both changes made the agents reject every answer, including correct ones. These patterns held in separate tests and in four consecutive reviews. The agents also rejected everything when logs remained incomplete, so we cannot attribute the end of their earlier approvals to restored evidence. Complete logs improved acceptance of correct work. These results show why a repair should be judged by both the errors it prevents and the useful work it preserves. Recovery was tested in one pair; deliberate coordination remains unproven.
